# Georgian cases (ბრუნვები)

Seven cases are used in modern writing (the vocative is rare in published copy). Endings for a consonant stem
(`სტუდენტი`), a vowel stem in -ა (`კომპანია`) and a non-truncating vowel stem (`ტოკიო`, `მეტრო`).

| Case | Georgian name | Consonant stem | -ა stem | -ო/-უ stem | Main use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | სახელობითი | სტუდენტი | კომპანია | ტოკიო | subject in series I; object in II and III |
| Ergative | მოთხრობითი | სტუდენტმა | კომპანიამ | ტოკიომ | subject of transitive/active verbs in series II |
| Dative | მიცემითი | სტუდენტს | კომპანიას | ტოკიოს | object in series I; indirect object; subject of feeling verbs and series III |
| Genitive | ნათესაობითი | სტუდენტის | კომპანიის | ტოკიოს | possession; separate postpositions |
| Instrumental | მოქმედებითი | სტუდენტით | კომპანიით | ტოკიოთი | means ("with, by"); base for -დან |
| Adverbial | ვითარებითი | სტუდენტად | კომპანიად | ტოკიოდ | "as, into"; base for -მდე |
| Vocative | წოდებითი | სტუდენტო | კომპანიავ | — | addressing someone |

Notes that models get wrong:

- `-ით` is the **instrumental** (მოქმედებითი), not the adverbial. `-ად` is the adverbial (ვითარებითი).
- An -ა stem loses the -ა before -ის and -ით: `კომპანია → კომპანიის, კომპანიით` (❌ `კომპანიაის`).
- Stems with a syncopating vowel drop it in some cases: `წყალი → წყლის, წყლით`; `მკვლევარი → მკვლევრის,
  მკვლევრები, მკვლევრებმა` (❌ `მკვლევარები`); `ქვეყანა → ქვეყნის, ქვეყნები`; `ფანჯარა → ფანჯრის`.
- Plural is `-ები` + case ending: `კომპანიები, კომპანიებმა, კომპანიებს, კომპანიების, კომპანიებით`.
  The `-ებ-` plural dominates, but the old plural `-ნი/-თა` is required in four places: some pronouns have only
  this plural (`ესენი`, `ისინი`, `მათ`); in set expressions it is the basic form (`ბავშვთა ოთახი`, `მშობელთა კრება`,
  `მონაცემთა ბაზა`); action nouns form a plural only with it (`მღერანი`, `ცქერანი`); cardinal numerals form only
  this plural (`ორნი`, `სამნი`, `შვიდნი`). (Peikrishvili §16)
- Suffix postpositions attach to a case form, not to the bare word:
  - dative stem (no final -ს): `კომპანია + ში = კომპანიაში`, `სერვერ + ზე = სერვერზე`
  - genitive: `კომპანიის + თვის = კომპანიისთვის`, `სერვერის + გან = სერვერისგან`
  - instrumental: `სერვერით → სერვერიდან`, `2024 წლით → 2024 წლიდან`
  - adverbial: `ქალაქად → ქალაქამდე`, `წელად → წლამდე`

## Gender and number (book rules)

- The Georgian noun has only case and number, and no grammatical gender. Attempts to mark the feminine with -ა or -ნა (-ინა), such as `მეფ-ა`, `მთავრ-ინა`, were never accepted in living speech or in written Georgian. (Peikrishvili §10, §12)
- The -ებ- plural has all seven cases and is the dominant plural today. The short plural has five: -ნ in the nominative and vocative, and -თ in the ergative, dative and genitive (the book's table writes -თა; its sentences also show bare -თ). It has no instrumental or adverbial, so those take -ებ-: `ბავშვნი`, `ბავშვთა`, `ბავშვნო`, `ბავშვებით`. In modern Georgian the -თ plural serves mostly as a genitive and less often as a dative (usually bare -თ there), and the -ნ form has almost gone. The book cites B. Sabashvili for a tendency towards -თა in the genitive and -თ in the dative. (Peikrishvili §16)
- Doubled letters in the old plural: a stem ending in ნ gets two ნ before -ნი, and a stem ending in თ gets two თ before -თა: `ბერძენნი`, `ბურთთა`. (Peikrishvili §32)
- A collective noun is singular in form, but in some uses it can also be plural in form and meaning: `ხალხი`, `გუნდი`, `ფარა`, `ერი`; `ხალხები`, `ერები`. (Peikrishvili §15)
- Personal (proper), geographic, abstract, material and action nouns generally have no plural, though they sometimes appear in the plural. (Peikrishvili §31)
  - A given name forms no plural, unless it stands for a literary type: `თამარის`, `ლუარსაბები`. (Peikrishvili §24, §31)
  - A place name declines like a common noun but has no plural: `ქუთაისის`. (Peikrishvili §24)
  - Consonant-final surnames keep the stem (except those in -ელი), decline like common nouns and form both plurals: `ბარათაშვილები`. (Peikrishvili §24)
  - Material nouns can have plural forms (`მარილები`) and so can action nouns, whose examples on the page are in -ნი (`მღერანი`). An action noun that has fully broken its link with the verb has both plurals: `შენიშვნები`, `შენიშვნანი`. (Peikrishvili §31)

## Personal names (book rules)

- Surnames in -ელი shorten like the nouns in `references/noun-stems.md` section 2: the ე drops before -ის, -ით, -ად and in the -ებ- plural, and stays in the nominative, ergative and dative: `წერეთლის`, `მაჩაბლის`, `წერეთლით`, `წერეთელმა`, `წერეთელს` (❌ `წერეთელის`, `მაჩაბელის`). (Peikrishvili §24, §25)
- When a first name and surname are declined together, only the surname takes the case ending; the first name stays as it is: `ილია ჭავჭავაძეს`. In a declined double surname the first part is the bare stem and only the last part declines. In a list the surname comes first, and a consonant-final first name then takes no -ი, since it is an address form: `აბაშიძე ვახტანგ`. (Peikrishvili §30)
- Vowel-final names mostly keep their vowel; native surnames in -ძე/-ე truncate. See `references/noun-stems.md` section 1. (Peikrishvili §27, §29)

## Vocative (book rules)

- A consonant-final given name in direct address is the bare stem, with no case ending and not -ი: `თამარ!`, `ნუგზარ!`, `ზურაბ!` (❌ `თამარი!`, `ზურაბი!`). A footnote adds that some dialects give these names -ო, as common nouns have. (Peikrishvili §24)
- A consonant-final surname in address takes the ending -ო, not -ი: `რატიანო!`, `გიგაურო!` (❌ `გიგაური!`). A place name also takes -ო (`ქუთაისო`), and the -ებ- plural takes -ებო (`ბავშვებო`). (Peikrishvili §24, §16)
- In the singular, the vocative sign of a vowel-final noun is -ო after a one-syllable stem and -ვ after a stem of two or more syllables: `ხეო`, `ზღვაო`, `რუო`; `ფანჯარავ`, `მოყვარევ`, `წყაროვ`. Verbal nouns have no vocative. (Peikrishvili §26, §27, §28)
- For personal names the literary norm is the bare stem: `ნონა`, `ივანე`, `გივი`. In lively speech, and through it in writing, a vocative sign sometimes appears (`ზურავ`, `პეტრევ`). A footnote adds that dialects also show the opposite trend: names in -ა of three or more syllables cut the final vowel. (Peikrishvili §29)
- Kvachadze agrees that a name in address is the bare stem. A vocative -ო (after a consonant) or -ვ (after a vowel) on a name is not rare in modern Georgian, but literary Georgian does not accept it: `ჩემო გაბრიელ!`, `ლუარსაბ!`, `ერეკლე, მოდი` (❌ `ერეკლევ`, `ზურავ`, `არჩილო`). (Kvachadze §155)
- Address-only and clipped forms: `დედილო` and `მამილო` serve only as address (often clipped to `დედი`, `მამი`), unlike other -ილ- nouns such as `ძმობილი`, which take every case. Kin words in -იკო (`დედიკო`, `ბიჭიკო`) are usually address forms, but decline once they work like a name (`დედიკომ`). Some nouns in -ა such as `ნათლიდედა`, and names in -ა or -ია, often drop that final ა in address: `ნათლიმამ!`, `ბიძინ!` (for `ბიძინა`). (Kvachadze §155)

## The particle ც and extended forms (book rules)

- On a word taken alone, the added ა after a dative, genitive, instrumental or adverbial ending is optional and mostly a matter of style: `სახლს` or `სახლსა`. The places below are where the book makes it required. (Peikrishvili §13)
- Put an ა between a dative, genitive, instrumental or adverbial ending and the particle ც, for common and proper nouns alike: `წიგნსაც`, `წიგნისაც`, `წიგნითაც`, `წიგნადაც`. (Peikrishvili §13)
- The dative before `-ვით` always takes the ა: `დედასავით`, `ბავშვსავით` (see Postpositions below). (Peikrishvili §13)
- The genitive-based dative (ნანათესაობითარი მიცემითი) is genitive ending + ა + ს: `ქალაქ-ის-ა-ს`, `ამხანაგ-ის-ა-ს`; the page names this form but does not explain when it is used. A governed genitive modifier placed after the noun it modifies takes a final ა: `ლექსი პოეტ-ის-ა`, `ბავშვი მეზობლ-ის-ა`. (Peikrishvili §13)
- Two nouns joined by `და` in the dative, genitive, instrumental or adverbial: the first takes the ა after its case ending, the second does not: `მთასა და ბარს`, `ცისა და დედამიწის`. The book lists this as required; its examples are dative and genitive only. (Peikrishvili §13) Kaeasy prefers the `-სა` form but does not mark `მთას და ბარს` as an error (Louie, 2026-11-07).
- Kvachadze describes the same pattern as usual: a noun right before `და` in the dative, genitive or instrumental usually has the extended ending (-სა, -ისა, -ითა), and the one after `და` the ordinary ending: `მეგობრებსა და ნათესავებს`, `დედისა და მამის`, `ხელითა და ფეხით`. With several attributes before one ergative or dative noun, each attribute before `და` can carry the full ending; in the dative the book's examples show -სა there, while the attribute next to the noun stays short: `ლამაზსა და პატიოსან ქერივს`. (Kvachadze §139, §145)
- A genitive attribute is normally short (-ის) and stays unchanged as its noun declines: `ტყის ჰაერი`, `დარბაზის სარკმელი`. The long -ისა turns up elsewhere only rarely, mostly in verse. Right before `და` it is usually long: of two genitives joined by `და`, the first takes -ისა and the second stays short: `ვერცხლისა და ოქროს კოვზები`. (Kvachadze §107)
- A vowel-final noun has no nominative ending (the old -ი lives on only in dialects). Its ergative sign is -მ, which becomes -მა before the particle -ც; its adverbial sign is -დ: `დედა`, `დედამ`, `დედამაც`, `ზღვად`, `ხედ`. (Peikrishvili §26)

## Spelling at the case ending (book rules)

- A stem ending in მ gets two მ in the ergative: `ლომმა`. A stem ending in ს gets two ს in the dative: `მაისს`. A stem in -ია gets two ი in the genitive and the instrumental: `ისტორიის`, `ისტორიით`. (Peikrishvili §32)
- Write the adverbial sign with `დ`, never `თ`. Consonant stems take -ად and vowel stems take -დ: `კაცად`, `ქალად`, `მდინარედ` (❌ `კაცათ`, `ქალათ`, `მდინარეთ`). (Peikrishvili §32.4)

## Postpositions (book rules)

- A postposition goes on the end of a case form. Nominative: `-ვით`, on consonant-final nouns only. Dative: `-ვით`, `-თან`, `-ზე`, `-ში`. Genitive: `-თვის`, `-გან`, `-კენ`, `-ებრ`, `-თანავე`, `-და`, `-დამი`; `-ვით` is rare there and found in dialects, and `-ებრ` can also go directly on the stem. Instrumental: `-ურთ`, rare in today's literary language, and `-გან`, whose modern form ends in `-იდან`. Adverbial: `-მდის`, `-მდე`. `-დამ` is out of use in today's literary language. Examples: `ლომივით`, `სახლში`, `სახლისკენ`, `სოფლიდან`, `სახლამდე`. (Peikrishvili §35-40, §42)
- Particles (`-კი`, `-ც(ა)`, `-ღა`, `-მე`, `-რე` and the like) go on a finished word, and its case makes no difference to them: they attach to any case, including the nominative and ergative, which take no postposition, and to words that do not decline. A postposition needs a set case, usually one and sometimes two, and goes with no other: `-ზე`, `-ში`, `-თან` take the dative; `-გან` the genitive or the instrumental (today `-იდან`). A particle can follow a bare case form or a postposition. The vocative takes neither: `დავითმაც`, `დავითისაც`, `გუშინაც`, `დავითისგან`, `დავითზეც`. (Chikobava §38)
- `-დან` ("from, since") takes a linking -ი- after a consonant-final noun and after a vowel noun that truncates, and goes straight onto a vowel noun that keeps its vowel: `სოფლიდან`, `ზღვიდან`, `წყაროდან` (❌ `სოფლიდამ`). (Peikrishvili §39, §40)
- `-მდე` (also `-მდის`, "up to, until") goes on the adverbial form without its -დ: a consonant-final stem keeps the adverbial -ა- (`სახლამდე`, `ქუთაისამდე`), and a vowel-final stem takes `-მდე` directly (`ხემდე`, `სასაფლაომდე`). It marks an end point in place or time, an approximate number ("up to, about") and the nominal part of a predicate: `ყივილამდე`, `სალამომდე`, `ოცდაათამდე კაცი`, `ერთ მილიონამდე`. (Peikrishvili §40; Kvachadze §120)
- With `-ზე` and `-ში` the dative -ს is dropped for all nouns; only two pronouns keep it (`მასში`, `ვისში`, `მასზე`, `ვისზე`). With `-თან` the -ს stays after a vowel stem and is dropped after a consonant stem: `სახლზე`, `ძმასთან`, `სახლთან`. (Peikrishvili §37)
- `-ვით` ("like"): on the nominative it attaches only to consonant-final nouns, and the ი before it is the nominative ending. On the dative it attaches to both consonant-final and vowel-final nouns, and the dative form is then always the extended one, ending in `-სავით`: `ლომივით`, `ბავშვივით`, `ლომსავით`, `ხესავით`. In dialects, and through them in writing, `-ვით` can attach to verbs and other parts of speech with an approximating sense, and the page says such forms are gaining ground. (Peikrishvili §36-38)
- `-თვის`, `-გან` and `-კენ` each attach to both the extended genitive (with -ა) and the short genitive, so both are correct, and the page gives no general instruction to choose one: `ძმისაგან`, `ძმისგან`, `სწავლისათვის`, `სოფლისაკენ`, `სახლისკენ`. Only for `-გან` does it make a forecast: the short form has the better prospects, provided the functions of the two variants do not split apart (a view credited to Chikobava). `-გან` marks the real doer, the cause, or the material something is made from, and it answers ვისგან? / რისგან?; for that reason the page calls `ამხანაგიდან მომივიდა წერილი` a mistake, because a letter from a person needs the `-გან` form. (Peikrishvili §38)
- `-დამი` is a compound postposition listed with the genitive. The page says it can occur today with forms of all three persons; its noun example is `ამხანაგისადმი`. A bare `-და`, which meant the same as today's `-თვის`, is now rare: it survives in a few expressions and in the Gurian dialect: `ჩემდამი`, `ჩემდა თავად`, `ამისდა მიუხედავად`. Forms of the `შვილისადა` type no longer occur. (Peikrishvili §38)
- Doubled letters before a postposition: a stem ending in ზ gets two ზ before `-ზე` (`კოვზზე`); a stem ending in შ gets two შ before `-ში` (`ბალიშში`); the page says the word with `-თან` should have two თ after a stem in თ (`ბურთთან`); `-ებრ` on a stem in -ე brings two ე together (`მზეებრ`). (Peikrishvili §41.1-4)
- When nouns joined by `და` are in the dative or genitive, the postposition goes on the later noun, and the noun before `და` usually has none (in the page's examples it keeps its case ending with -ა): `დედისა და შვილისათვის`, `წარსულსა და მომავალზე`. In the instrumental and the adverbial the noun before `და` takes the postposition too: `შუბლიდან და ცხვირიდან`, `ეზომდე და სახლამდე`. (Peikrishvili §41.5-6)
- A title of a book, journal, newspaper or work that stands in the nominative declines, and takes a postposition when one is needed. The generic word before it sometimes takes the case too (`ჟურნალმა „მნათობმა"`) and sometimes does not (`გაზეთ „დროებაში"`, `პოემა „განდეგილზე"`). The page says it is better to leave a title unchanged in four situations: it is in another case form; it is a noun with a postposition, or a phrase whose last word has one; it is a sentence; or it is a head noun with a modifier. Then decline the generic word placed before it instead: `მოთხრობაში „სარჩობელაზედ"` (❌ `„სარჩობელაზედში"`). (Peikrishvili §41.7-8)

## Compounds (book rules)

- A compound is one word even when it holds two or three stems, so it declines as one word: the case ending comes once, after the last part, and the first part stays a bare stem: `ქართლ-კახეთი`, `ქართლ-კახეთმა`, `ქართლ-კახეთს`. Paired compounds are hyphenated (`ცოლ-შვილი`, `სახლ-კარი`); modifier-plus-noun compounds are written solid (`სულმოკლე`, `ფერწასული`). (Chikobava §68)
- Genitive plus noun, one word or two? Try swapping the two or putting a word between them. If that breaks the meaning, it is a compound, written as one word: `ჯარისკაცი`, `ქინძისთავი`, `ციხისძირი`, `თავისუფლება`. If it works freely, it is a two-word phrase: `მეზობლის სახლი`, `მეზობლის ახალი სახლი`. If it works but sounds awkward, the pair is in between, and the book records both spellings (`ცოლის ძმა`, solid or apart). (Chikobava §68)

## Latin names (brands) with case endings

The hyphen is mandatory; the ending follows how the name **sounds**:

| Name | Ends in sound | Ergative | Dative | Genitive | Instrumental | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft | consonant | Microsoft-მა | Microsoft-ს | Microsoft-ის | Microsoft-ით | — |
| Google | consonant | Google-მა | Google-ს | Google-ის | Google-ით | — |
| OpenAI | vowel (…აი) | OpenAI-მ | OpenAI-ს | OpenAI-ს | OpenAI-ით | OpenAI-ები |
| ChatGPT | vowel (…ტი) | ChatGPT-მ | ChatGPT-ს | ChatGPT-ს | ChatGPT-ით | ChatGPT-ები |
| NVIDIA | vowel (…ა) | NVIDIA-მ | NVIDIA-ს | NVIDIA-ს | NVIDIA-თი | — |

Decided 2026-11-02 (`docs/NEEDS-REVIEW.md`): names ending in `-ა`, `-ო`, `-უ` take `-ს` in the genitive (`Meta-ს`, `NVIDIA-ს`, `Tesla-ს`, as Georgian media write them) and `-თი` in the instrumental (`NVIDIA-თი`, `GPU-თი`, never `NVIDIA-ით`). The fleet's older `Meta-ის` is retired.

All foreign names ending in a vowel sound (-ა, -ე, -ი sound, -ო, -უ) take **`-მ`** in the ergative, **`-ს`** in the dative and genitive, and **`-თი`** in the instrumental (`Meta-ს`, `Tesla-ს`, `NVIDIA-ს`, `OpenAI-ს`, `ChatGPT-ს`, `Sony-ს`, `GPU-ს`; instrumental `NVIDIA-თი`, `GPU-თი`), except the -ი sound, which takes **`-ით`** like `ტაქსით` (`OpenAI-ით`, `ChatGPT-ით`). Consonant stems strictly take **`-მა`**, **`-ს`**, **`-ის`**, **`-ით`** (`Microsoft-ის`, `Google-ის`). For the complete 7-case paradigm and 200+ brand dictionary, see `references/latin-names.md` in `kaeasy:georgian-names-numbers`.
